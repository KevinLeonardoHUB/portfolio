ARMIAC — Intelligent Robotic Hand

A functional articulated robotic-hand prototype, 3D printed and controlled by a Flutter app through Wi-Fi, HTTP and voice commands.

About the project

ARMIAC integrates mobile development, embedded systems, electronics, networking and 3D manufacturing. The Android app sends HTTP requests to the ESP32, which communicates over I2C with the PCA9685 and turns commands into PWM signals for the servomotors.

Features

  • Individual finger and forearm control
  • Open/close hand and predefined gestures
  • Portuguese voice commands
  • Bilingual interface, tutorial and connection status
  • Articulated PLA-printed structure

Technologies

Flutter Dart C++/Arduino ESP32 PCA9685 Wi‑Fi/HTTP I2C/PWM 3D Printing

Challenges and learning

The work required learning Flutter and Dart, calibrating servos, stabilising power, adjusting tendons and mechanical parts, and integrating software with real hardware.

Future vision

I want to continue developing ARMIAC as resources and funding become available. Alongside its educational purpose, I intend to explore applications related to accessibility and communication in learning environments.

  • A second robotic hand and arm: build and synchronise two arms to reproduce a wider range of gestures.
  • Speech and sign language: research an experimental system that receives spoken input, for example during a lesson, and reproduces corresponding signs. This goal requires research into speech processing, sign-language interpretation and mechanical articulation; it is not yet implemented.
  • Precision and durability: add finger pressure sensors, improve servo calibration, make movements smoother, and reinforce the structure and internal cable management.
  • Controls and application: create custom movement profiles, automated programmed movements and command history, as well as explore iOS support.
  • Communication and portability: explore Bluetooth connectivity and a battery solution better suited to transporting the prototype.
